I am a Bolivian economist working on development economics and international trade. I am currently completing an MSc in Economics with a specialization in econometrics at the University of Geneva.

My work combines applied econometrics, survey data and reproducible workflows to produce evidence that is useful for policy. I am particularly interested in poverty, education, labor markets and trade policy in developing countries.

I recently interned at the Trade Policy Review Division of the World Trade Organization, where I built R and Python pipelines for trade indicators used in Trade Policy Reviews. Before that, I managed data collection for an IDB-funded impact evaluation in Guyana and worked as a research assistant at the Research Centre in Economics and Entrepreneurship (CIEE) at Universidad Privada Boliviana.

My research has been published in [Health Economics](https://doi.org/10.1002/hec.4931) and in a CEPR eBook chapter on who pays for US tariffs, co-authored with Marcelo Olarreaga. I hold a BSc in Economics from Universidad Privada Boliviana.
